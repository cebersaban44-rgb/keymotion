**Settings** holds how the app looks and which language it uses, what happens when you close the window, the keyboard's color tuning, backups, updates and the ways to get help. Open it at the bottom of the sidebar.

<Screenshot name="settings" alt="The top of the Settings page: Appearance, and Closing and starting with its two switches" />

## Appearance

- **Theme**: **Light**, **Dark** or **System**. **System** follows Windows' light or dark mode, and is what Keymotion starts with.
- **Language**: **Türkçe** or **English**. The whole app changes at once, including the menu of its icon next to the clock. Keymotion starts in Turkish when Windows is in Turkish, and in English otherwise.

## Closing and starting

Lighting, key mapping, macros and the picture on the screen are saved on the keyboard itself, so they work with Keymotion closed. The focus timer, reminders, calendar cards, the live screen and the gauges on the keys need Keymotion running.

- **Keep running after closing the window** is on at first. Closing the window then leaves Keymotion running, with its icon at the bottom right of the screen, next to the clock. The first time you close the window after Keymotion starts, a Windows notification says it is **Still running**.
  - To open the window again, click the icon, or open Keymotion again from the Start menu.
  - To quit, right-click the icon and choose **Quit**.
  - If you can't see the icon, click the **^** arrow next to the clock.
  - With this switch off, closing the window quits Keymotion.
- **Start Keymotion when the computer starts** is off at first. With it on, Keymotion starts when you sign in to Windows, without opening its window; only its icon next to the clock appears. If Keymotion is turned off in Windows' own startup settings, this switch shows off and says so; you can turn it on again here.

The icon's right-click menu also shows the keyboard and its battery, runs the **Focus timer** (start, pause, skip to the next phase, stop), shows the next reminder, turns **Reminders** and **System gauges on the keys** on or off, lists your **Profiles**, and has **Open Keymotion**. Choosing a profile there opens its load confirmation on the [Profiles](/keymotion/guide/profiles/) page; nothing is saved until you confirm.

If you quit while some changes aren't saved to the keyboard, Keymotion asks first.

## Color calibration

**Color calibration** brings the keys' colors closer to what you see on your screen. Your changes are kept as you make them; **Restore defaults** undoes them.

- **Gamma** sets how dark the mid tones are. **Gamma per color** gives red, green and blue a slider each.
- **White balance** sets the highest brightness of each color.
- **Test pattern** lights the keyboard **White**, in **Gray steps** or with the app's **Palette**, so you can compare it with the drawing under **Compare with your keyboard**. It needs the USB cable, and your saved lighting comes back when you leave the page.

Calibration applies to previews and to the colors you save from now on. To update the colors already on the keyboard, save them again on the [Lighting](/keymotion/guide/lighting/) page.

## Backup

- **Export settings**, then **Export**, saves your settings, key mapping, macros and library in one file in your Downloads folder. **Show in folder** opens the folder. If you have added calendars, **Include calendar addresses** also puts their addresses in the file.
- **Restore from a backup**, then **Choose file**, shows what each part of the file will change before anything happens. Choose the parts and click **Restore**. Nothing is written to the keyboard: lighting, key mapping and macros open as unsaved changes, which you save on their own pages.

<Aside type="caution" title="Calendar addresses work like passwords">
With **Include calendar addresses** on, the addresses are saved in the file in plain text. Anyone who has the file can read those calendars, so keep it private.
</Aside>

## Updates

The **About** section starts with the **Version** row: the version you have and what Keymotion found the last time it looked, for example that it is up to date. **Check for updates** looks on GitHub right away.

With **Check for updates automatically** on, which it is at first, Keymotion looks once a day, a short while after it opens. It never installs anything by itself.

When a new version is out, a strip above every page says it is ready. **Later** hides the strip until Keymotion starts again.

<Steps>

1. Click **Install now**, on the strip or in the **Version** row.
2. Read **What's new**. If the notes aren't available in your language, **Release notes on GitHub** opens them in your browser.
3. Click **Install and restart**. Keymotion downloads the new version; **Cancel** stops the download.
4. If something is being saved to the keyboard, Keymotion waits for it to finish. Then it closes, the installer shows its progress in a small window, and Keymotion opens again.

</Steps>

<Screenshot name="update-dialog" alt="The window for installing a new version, with the version you use, the release date, the list of what's new, and Install and restart" />

The download is checked before it is installed. If you have changes that aren't saved to the keyboard, the window warns that installing loses them. If something goes wrong, Keymotion says what happened, such as no internet connection or a download that couldn't be verified, and **Try again** looks once more.

## Report a problem

<Steps>

1. In **About**, next to **Feedback**, click **Report a problem**.
2. The **Report the problem** window shows **What will be sent**: the app and Windows versions, the keyboard and its connection, and the latest lines of Keymotion's log. Web addresses and your Windows user name are left out.
3. Click **Report on GitHub**. The details go to the clipboard, and the project's problem form opens in your browser. You need a GitHub account to send it.
4. Describe what happened, paste the details into the form's details box with Ctrl+V, and send the form.

</Steps>

<Screenshot name="report-dialog" alt="The Report the problem window with the details that will be sent, and the Copy and Report on GitHub buttons" />

**Copy** puts the same text on the clipboard without opening GitHub, if you'd rather send it another way. Keymotion itself never sends anything.

Keymotion also offers this window on its own. After an unexpected error, a notice says **Something unexpected went wrong**, with **Report the problem**. After a crash, the next start shows **Keymotion ran into a problem last time**, with **Report**, once.

## The rest of About

<Screenshot name="settings-about" alt="The About section of Settings: Version, Check for updates automatically, Window look, Feedback, Diagnostics, Log files, Data folder and Privacy" />

- **Suggest an idea**, next to **Feedback**, opens the project's idea form on GitHub.
- **Diagnostics**, then **Copy**, puts the same kind of report on the clipboard, for a message of your own. Calendar addresses and personal details are left out.
- **Log files** and **Data folder**, each with **Open folder**, open the folder with the last seven days of logs and any crash notes, and the folder that holds your settings and library.
- **Window look** shows **Translucent (Windows 11)** or **Solid**. Keymotion picks it to suit your version of Windows; there is nothing to set.
- **Privacy** says that the app doesn't collect or send usage data, and goes online only to read the calendars you add and to check GitHub for a new version. [Privacy](/keymotion/about/privacy/) has the details.
